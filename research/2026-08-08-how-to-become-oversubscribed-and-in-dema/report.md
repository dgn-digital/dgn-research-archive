# Research Synthesis: "How to Become Oversubscribed and In Demand"

## A Note on Source Quality First

Before synthesising, a critical caveat: **only 4 of your 8 videos are actually relevant to your business.** Videos 3, 5, 6, and 8 are financial-news reports about IPOs and capital placements (SpaceX, StubHub, SK Hynix, G50 Corp) where "oversubscribed" means *investor demand exceeding shares available* — a capital-markets term, not a customer-acquisition strategy. They tell you nothing actionable about selling heating services or AI care plans to trades.

Videos 1, 2, and 4 (plus a fragment of the concept in others) cover the relevant material: Daniel Priestley's *Oversubscribed* methodology applied to service businesses. I'll flag throughout where I'm drawing on genuinely applicable evidence versus noise.

I cannot independently verify the specific claims attributed to Daniel Priestley's book, nor the seminar/concert figures cited — treat them as claims made in the videos, not established facts.

---

## 1. PATTERNS — What Shows Up Repeatedly

**The core mechanism (Videos 1, 2, 4):** Demand is manufactured *before* supply is offered. Every relevant video describes the same sequence:
- Build visibility/content → collect signals of interest → validate with a waitlist → open a capacity-capped offer → sell out → convert overflow via follow-up.

**Genuine (not fake) scarcity via capacity caps (Videos 1, 4):** Both stress that scarcity must be *real*. Video 4 is explicit: "The days of creating fake scarcity are gone." Video 1 warns: "Do not expand capacity or raise prices mid-campaign." The credibility of the model rests on the cap being honest.

**The waitlist-before-you-build pattern (Videos 2, 4):** Video 2 cites 150+ signups as a "go" signal; Video 4 cites a book example of 600 Facebook sign-ups for a 200-seat event. Validate demand before committing resources.

**Trust and visibility as foundations (Videos 1, 2):** Video 2 names trust as "the foundation." Video 1's 7/11/4 framework (7 hours of content, 11 interactions, 4 platforms) is a trust-accumulation model. This directly aligns with your **trust-first, anti-hype positioning.**

**Overflow ≠ waste — it's the pipeline (Videos 1, 4):** Unsold demand is converted through follow-up sales calls (Video 1) or fed forward to the next campaign. Video 1's lead-tier ratios (5x hot, 10x warm, 100x cold capacity) formalise this.

**In the finance videos**, the only transferable pattern is that oversubscription is used *as social proof* ("evidence of global investor demand," Video 6) — demand begets demand.

---

## 2. OUTLIERS — Who Does Something Different

**Atiba de Souza (Video 4)** runs a two-step funnel with an unusually tight cap: a free ~1hr web class (75–100 attendees) feeding a **paid 4-hour ICP workshop capped at 8 people.** That's a ~90%+ intentional exclusion rate. Whether it "works" is unverifiable — no conversion rate or workshop price is disclosed. But the *structure* is sound and directly copyable for a premium service.

**The finance videos as a group** are outliers to your question entirely — but SpaceX (Videos 3 & 5) shows an interesting deviation: **fixed single-price issuance with no range** ("none of this priced at bottom or top of range"). The transferable idea: removing pricing negotiation/ambiguity can itself be a positioning move. **This is thin evidence** and I wouldn't lean on it.

**Video 1's seminar example** (93 pre-registrations for 60 seats, 47 sold on open) is the only relevant video offering *actual conversion numbers* — but it's the presenter's own claim with no verification.

## 4. GAPS & OPPORTUNITIES — What Nobody Covers

**Every relevant video defers the hard part.** Video 1 admits "how to build the 7/11/4 content stack is deferred to a separate video." Video 4 poses "how to get there" as an unanswered question. Video 2 offers the 150-signup threshold "without sourcing." **The mechanism of demand-creation is described everywhere; the execution is explained nowhere.** That's your opening.

**Opportunities DGN could own:**

- **The trades-specific oversubscription playbook.** Nobody here applies this to trade businesses. A gas engineer using a waitlist + capacity cap is novel in a market where trades typically "launch and hope."
- **Proof-of-concept from your own business.** You can *run* the Oversubscribed model on DGN Gas (a genuinely capacity-constrained premium service), document the results, and sell that documented method through DGN Digital. This is a defensible moat: you're not a marketer *talking about* oversubscription — you're a trade owner who *did it.*
- **Honest scarcity as anti-hype differentiator.** Video 4's "fake scarcity is gone" point aligns perfectly with your positioning. A trade with genuinely limited install slots has *real* scarcity — no manufacturing required.
- **AI-powered lead nurture for the 7/11/4 stack.** The videos never explain how to sustain content/interactions at scale. Your AI content agents and lead-capture tools are *literally the missing execution layer.* This is a product-market fit signal.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

**[ADOPT NOW] — Run the Oversubscribed model on DGN Gas as your proof asset.**
DGN Gas has genuine, honest capacity limits (a premium heating firm can only take X installs per quarter). Publish visible availability ("2 install slots remaining this month"), operate a waitlist, and refuse to over-expand. Document everything. This costs almost nothing and produces the case study DGN Digital needs. Evidence: Videos 1 & 4's capacity-cap model applies most cleanly to exactly this kind of business.

**[ADOPT NOW] — Reposition DGN Digital's AI tools as the "execution layer" for demand-building.**
Every relevant video leaves the *how* unexplained (Videos 1, 2, 4 all defer it). Frame your content agents, booking tools, and lead capture as the machinery that makes the 7/11/4 visibility stack actually achievable for a busy trade. This is a genuine gap, not hype.

**[TEST] — A capped, productised entry offer feeding a premium care plan.**
Mirror Video 4's structure: a free/low-cost feeder (e.g. a webinar or audit for trades) → a capacity-capped premium onboarding cohort. Test the cap size and conversion. Do NOT copy the "8 seats" number — that's arbitrary to de Souza's business.

**[TEST] — Waitlist-before-build for any new AI add-on.**
Before developing the next AI feature, put up a waitlist and use a signup threshold as your build/no-build gate (Video 2's method). Set *your own* threshold from your list economics — the "150" figure is unsourced (Video 2's own gap) and not a benchmark for you.

**[WATCH] — Oversubscription-as-social-proof mechanics.**
The finance videos (6, 8) show demand signals amplifying demand. Watch whether publicly displaying your waitlist/scarcity (Video 1's "visible waiting list") actually lifts conversion for trades, who may respond differently to urgency than affluent homeowners. Monitor before scaling.

---

## 6. CONTRADICTIONS & CAUTIONS

**Fake vs. real scarcity — a live tension.** Video 4 declares fake scarcity dead, while Video 1's "make the waiting list visible to other leads" edges toward *engineered* social proof. The distinction matters for your anti-hype brand: display scarcity only when it's genuinely true. Get this wrong and it becomes exactly the hype you position against.

**Unverified and self-promotional claims — treat with scepticism:**
- Video 1's seminar numbers (93/60/47) are the presenter's own unverified claims.
- Video 2 characterises Priestley as building "multiple businesses to millions" — explicitly the host's characterisation, not first-hand, and no mentee outcomes are stated.
- Video 4's "75–100 attendees" and the Wu-Tang/watch-market examples are illustrative anecdotes, not evidence the method scales.
- Video 2's 150-signup threshold is admitted to be unsourced.

**The finance videos are a category error for your purpose.** Do not import "oversubscribed 7x" or "$160B demand" thinking into a services strategy — these are capital-market events with mechanics (underwriters, allocations, roadshows) that have no analogue in selling care plans. Their only useful transfer is the *psychological* principle that visible demand attracts more demand.

**The biggest caution: none of this is validated for trades.** Every relevant source demonstrates the model in coaching/consulting/events — audiences primed to buy "growth." Trade business owners are famously sceptical of marketing and time-poor. The model is *plausible* for your context and aligns with your positioning, but the videos provide **zero evidence it works in your specific market.** That's precisely why Recommendation 1 (prove it on your own DGN Gas first) matters — it converts an unproven claim into your own verifiable asset.

## TACTICS
- **7/11/4 framework:** prospects must accumulate 7 hours with content, 11 interactions, across 4 platforms/locations before offer opens
- **Lead quality tiers with ratios:**
  - Hot leads (time + money spent) → need **5x capacity**
  - Warm leads (time or money, not both) → need **10x capacity**
  - Cold leads (email, click, follow) → need **100x capacity**
- Make waiting list **visible to other leads** (social proof of scarcity)
- State qualifying terms ("if you want to work with us, you need to be this kind of person") before selling
- Run ~**4 major campaigns per year**
- Post-launch: sales calls to convert remaining warm leads
- **Do not expand capacity or raise prices mid-campaign**

---

## PROOF
- Presenter claims personal experience running a training company with failed last-minute event promotion
- Seminar example cited: 93 pre-registrations for 60 seats; 47 tickets sold immediately on opening
