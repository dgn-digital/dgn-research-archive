## 5. MECHANISMS

*The reader holds the principles — below is the executable machine.*

**M1 — Content-Minutes Offer-Ladder Map** (V1, asserted)
1. List your offers low→high price.
2. Assign a content-minutes threshold per tier: ~20 min for a ~$47 offer; hundreds of minutes for high-ticket.
3. Choose long-form (20–60 min watch time) as primary trust vehicle — 5 episodes in a night ≈ 200 min.
4. Add short-form only after long-form formula is proven.
→ *Produces:* a trust-threshold model mapping content volume to purchase readiness. *No real data shown.*

**M2 — Three-Metrics Priority Stack** (V1, asserted)
1. Track #1 Conversions (emails/leads per video, via ManyChat CTA on every video).
2. Track #2 Followers gained per video (weighted 5–10× a view).
3. Track #3 Total views (benchmarking only).
→ *Produces:* a measurement hierarchy that de-prioritises vanity reach. *Warning:* ManyChat comment-automation can shade into mass automated outreach if used to DM at scale — disclose and keep human.

**M3 — Topic-Sourcing Ratio (Testing Batches)** (V1, asserted)
1. Produce videos in batches of 10.
2. Source 70–80% of topics from proven competitor performers; 20% original tests.
3. First 20–30 videos = validation; identify winning topic buckets.
4. Then shift to your own channel's data as primary signal.
→ *Produces:* a de-risked topic pipeline. *No channel-level data shown.*

**M4 — Sandcastles Research Workflow** (V1, tool-demoed on screen)
1. Channels tab → "Describe" your niche → filter follower range 10K–250K (avoid 1M+; their outliers are brand-driven).
2. Add ~20 channels; use double-arrow "anchor" to surface lookalikes.
3. Videos tab → filter "posted last 2 months" (4–6 for evergreen; 7–14 days for news) → min engagement 2% → sort by Outlier Score (views ÷ creator's rolling 3-mo average).
4. "Analyze" a video → extract hook text, contrarian angle, structure, evidence.
→ *Produces:* a shortlist of proven, above-average-performing topic/hook templates. *Caveat:* tool capabilities are vendor-demonstrated, not independently verified.

**M5 — Fandom Funnel Thresholds** (V2, asserted)
1. Map viewer stages: 1 min (first view) → 5–10 min (follower) → 20–30 min (casual fan) → 30–60 min (active supporter) → hundreds of min (super fan).
2. Plan content volume to move cohorts up each threshold.
→ *Produces:* a staged nurture model. *Conversion claim (5–10 super fans / 1,000 viewers) is asserted, no data.*

**M6 — Claude + Sandcastles MCP Topic Heatmap** (V2, process described) — **most relevant to Lens 7**
1. Pull top 30–50 videos from your channel or 1–2 close competitors.
2. Get transcripts.
3. Feed into Claude; prompt for a topic distribution / heat map.
4. Install Sandcastles.ai MCP into Claude (~30-sec setup claimed) for direct social-data access.
5. Repeat every 2–4 weeks before each content batch.
→ *Produces:* a data-backed content plan generated inside Claude. *Caveat:* MCP setup ease is asserted; verify security/data-handling before connecting any third-party MCP to a business Claude instance.

**M7 — Proof-in-Line-2/3** (V2 Shift 6, asserted)
1. Line 1 = hook.
2. Lines 2–3 = inject proof (result you got, experiment run, case study, hours spent).
3. Filter every piece: "Why should the viewer trust this?" — no answer → add proof.
→ *Produces:* early credibility that lifts retention. *Warning:* only use real proof — fabricated results/case studies would be deceptive.

**M8 — Connective Tissue / Binge Routing** (V2 Shift 5, asserted)
1. Long-form: in-video cards + verbal "if you liked this, watch this next" at end.
2. Short-form: numbered series + "link video" button + story-highlight playlists; do NOT say "watch next" verbally.
→ *Produces:* increased session content-minutes (removes viewer's next-choice decision).

**M9 — Embedded Truths Rewrite** (V3, script-level, demonstrated)
1. Find every *if, maybe, might, could, probably* in your script.
2. Replace with *when, the reason why, once you do this.*
3. "This might work because" → "The reason this works so well."
→ *Produces:* more authoritative copy. **⚠ Caution:** this converts hedged, uncertain claims into stated certainties. For DGN GAS (gas safety, regulated work) and anti-hype positioning, **over-applying this fabricates certainty** — a direct conflict with trust-first branding and potentially with advertising-standards honesty. Use only where the certainty is genuinely true.

**M10 — Loop Openers / Re-hook Cadence** (V3, demonstrated)
1. Re-hook every 20–30 sec (short-form) / 60–90 sec (long-form).
2. After each section insert a line that validates + teases next.
3. Always include a contrast word (but/however).
4. 5-section video → 3–4 loop openers. Diagnostic: steady retention decay = missing loops.
→ *Produces:* higher watch-through. **⚠ Caution:** "but that's not even the most interesting part" can become manufactured suspense — keep the payoff real.

**M11 — Contrast-Word Haymakers** (V3, demonstrated)
1. Find clean main-point sentences; split in half.
2. Lean viewer to assumption A, redirect to B using but / actually / instead / turns out / except / yet.
→ *Produces:* sharper, more persuasive lines.

**M12 — Term Branding** (V3, conceptual only)
1. Name the core concept before explaining it.
2. Prioritise naming contrarian ideas and best frameworks.
→ *Produces:* memorable, ownable IP. *Note: the how-to-name process is not taught.*

**M13 — Negative Frames** (V3, demonstrated)
1. Write the positive point.
2. Invert: "Use this hook" → "Stop making hooks like this."
3. Use when positive isn't landing. *Warning:* chronic negative-framing can read as fearmongering — off-brand for premium trust-first positioning.

## TACTICS

**Story Lock 1 — Term Branding**
1. Identify the core concept in your video
2. Give it a proper name (don't just explain it — brand it)
3. State the name before explaining it; this creates instant anticipation ("wait, what is that?")
4. Before publishing, ask: "Could I package this idea and give it a name?"
5. Prioritize naming contrarian ideas and best frameworks; not every concept needs it

**Story Lock 2 — Embedded Truths**
1. Open your script and find every instance of: *if, maybe, might, could, probably*
2. Replace with: *when, the reason why, once you do this, this is how it works*
3. Swap weak frames for certain ones:
   - "This might work because" → "The reason this works so well"
   - "You might notice this" → "Once you see it, you can't unsee it"
   - "Some creators might be making a mistake" → "The mistake most creators make is this"
4. Read back: if it sounds like hedging, rewrite it as a stated truth

**Story Lock 3 — Thought Narration**
1. After every major point in your script, stop and ask: "What should the viewer be thinking right now?"
2. At natural transition moments, say that thought out loud in the script
3. Use lead-ins: "Now, the question you're probably thinking is…" / "If I were you, the thing biggest on my mind right now is…" / "If there's one thing you're worried about, it's probably…"
4. Don't force it every sentence; a few times per video is sufficient

**Story Lock 4 — Negative Frames**
1. Write your point in its normal positive form
2. Apply the "negative flip" — invert it:
   - "Use this hook format" → "Stop making your hooks like this"
   - "Here's how to grow on YouTube" → "This is killing your YouTube growth"
   - "Post more consistently" → "This posting mistake is costing you followers"
3. Use negative frame when a point isn't landing; default to positive when it already hits hard
4. Amplifiers: triple negatives ("never, ever, ever") intensify the warning signal

**Story Lock 5 — Loop Openers**
