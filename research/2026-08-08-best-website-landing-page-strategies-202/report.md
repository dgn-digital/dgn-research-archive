## 1. PATTERNS — What Shows Up Repeatedly

**Offer/funnel structure (near-universal):**
- **Free lead magnet → paid product** sequence appears in almost every video (2, 4, 5, 6, 7). The free asset is consistently framed as a *trust-builder*, not an afterthought. Alex Cattoni (7) is most explicit: the free report exists "specifically to warm leads toward" the paid course.
- **Content-to-resource funnel**: video → gated download → product awareness (HubSpot in 2 and 6 do this identically).

**Landing page tactics (the genuine LP videos, 2/3/7):**
- **Lead with buyer benefits, not company copy** (2, 3).
- **Single primary CTA**, objections handled *before* the CTA (3), with dual placement top+bottom (7).
- **Above-fold completeness** — what it is / why you want it / how to get it visible without scrolling (7); price + buy button above fold (7).
- **Social proof at multiple touchpoints** (2, 3, 7 — testimonials, FAQ, authority banner, bio for cold traffic).
- **Reduce friction**: cut form fields, reduce clicks, add privacy/data statements (2, 5).
- **"Always be testing"** stated as a rule (2, 7).

**SEO/GEO consensus (videos 1, 4, 8, 9 strongly agree):**
- **GEO/AEO = good SEO, not a new discipline** (8 explicitly; 9 echoes "SEO is the foundation AI search is built on"). This is the single most consistent strategic claim across the whole set.
- **E-E-A-T via firsthand experience** — original photos, case studies with specific numbers, real author bylines, expert interviews (1, 8).
- **Topical clusters around money pages**, complete one cluster before starting the next (1, 9).
- **Answer-first formatting** — FAQs, bullet lists, tables, clear heading hierarchy so LLMs can quote you (6, 8, 9).
- **Roundup/"best of" article inclusion** as a citation source, even without dofollow links (1, 8, 9).

**Positioning language that recurs:**
- "There's a formula/repeatable process" (3, 1, 4) — used to signal systematisation.
- Anti-generic-agency framing (4 most sharply: agencies "charge clients, not get results").

## 6. CONTRADICTIONS & CAUTIONS

**Contradictions:**
- **Page length**: Video 2/3 imply thorough persuasive pages; Video 7 argues short pages for cheap offers. *Resolution:* both are right — page length should scale with price/risk. For DGN Gas (high-value, high-anxiety) → longer, more reassurance. For a DGN Digital tripwire → shorter.
- **Website's role**: Video 4 says the site is subordinate to the GBP; videos 1/9 treat the site as the primary asset. *Resolution:* depends on whether the query is local-map-driven (Ulku's world — highly relevant to DGN Gas) or informational (Surfer's world).

**Hype / weak-evidence cautions:**
- **Unsourced statistics are rampant.** "8 of 10 read the headline" (3), "attention span less than a goldfish" (3), "60% of searches end without a click" (6), "1 in 5 marketers" (6), "37% increase on Perplexity" (8, "unnamed 2024 study") — treat all as directional, not factual.
- **Self-promotional funnels distort advice.** Video 4 routes every tactic to a tool he's affiliated with or a service he sells; Surfer videos (1, 8, 9) promote Surfer at every workflow stage while never disclosing price. The *tactics* are sound; the *tool dependency* is a sales mechanism.
- **Fictional/absent proof.** Video 2 uses "HubSocks," an admitted fictional case study — no real results. Video 3 shows no actual pages or campaign data despite authority claims. Video 8's presenter claims "growth partner" status with *zero* client results.
- **Video 4's single strong result** (Plano plumber: position 17→2.35 in 2 weeks, $50k+ revenue) is a compelling but *unverifiable single anecdote* from a source with clear commercial incentive. I cannot verify Lead Snap, "Ice Cream Truck Shop," or the "Core 30" as established methods — treat as one practitioner's branded system, not industry standard.
- **A note on names I can't verify:** presenter identity appears inconsistently as "Matt Kenyon" (V1) vs "Matt Canyon" (V8) for what may be the same Surfer-affiliated presenter — I cannot confirm which is correct. "Source of Sources," "Lead Snap," and "365 Day Inbox Income" I cannot independently verify; I'm reporting them as claimed, not endorsing them.

**Bottom line for your positioning:** the anti-hype, trust-first stance you already hold is *validated* by how thin and self-promotional most of this material is. Your strongest unfair advantage — being a real trade operator who builds the AI systems — maps directly onto the one signal every credible source agrees matters most (firsthand experience E-E-A-T), and onto the biggest uncovered gap (CRO + AEO for premium local trades).

- Categories: use 2–10 relevant categories (tool: GMB Everywhere Chrome plugin + AI prompt); cross-reference list to avoid AI hallucinating fake categories

**Website: The Core 30 Structure**
- ~30 pages total: 1 homepage + 3–4 category pages + 25–30 service pages
- Homepage: 8 consistency signals (title tag = primary category + city; H1 matches; Google Maps embed; secondary categories in H2s with 50–100 words each; review widget; exact address match; exact phone match; local business schema)
- Each secondary category gets editorial link (in-content, not nav/footer) from homepage → category page
- Category pages: keyword = category + city in title/H1; services as H2s with 50–100 words + editorial links → service pages
- Service pages: keyword = service + city; detailed, specific, city-contextualized content
- Content built with AI but requires human edit: add images, callouts, tables, formatting, internal links; must not look like "wall of AI slop" on mobile Chrome

**Topical Relevance (below threshold)**

- Each supporting page = 1 external "not AI slop" link minimum

**Geographic Relevance (at/above threshold)**

- Identify landmarks visible in Google Maps: neighborhoods, parks, intersections, schools, shopping centers
- Target keyword: service + landmark + city → title tag + H1
- Content: genuinely local (common issues in that area, nearby references, driving routes); AI handles well
- Use rank map CSV data + AI prompt for content prioritization with demographic data

## TACTICS
**Case 1 – ICP/Messaging:**
- Pull reviews, call notes, community threads → feed into AI to surface buyer language patterns
- Write value prop in one sentence (specific enough to self-select)
- Document brand voice with enough specificity to prompt AI reliably (include tone, forbidden words, channel-specific shifts)

**Case 2 – Personalization:**
- Audit CRM + email tool + website analytics integration gaps
- Use behavioral triggers for automated list segmentation (form fills, email opens)
- Start with 2–3 broad segments
- Match lead magnet type to segment: cold = low-commitment (checklist), warm = decision-stage asset
- Personalize only subject line, opener, and CTA; use AI to rewrite per segment

**Case 3 – AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) — the "mystery tactic":**
- Audit traffic sources (last 90 days) to measure zero-click search impact
- Repurpose one asset across YouTube, LinkedIn, communities, email — no new content required
- AEO structure: **answer first, keywords second** (inverse of SEO)
- Best formats: FAQ (short Q + 1–2 sentence answer), scannable headers, clear blog/video descriptions
- Target: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Google AI Overviews
- Warm outbound: content presence in AI answers/Reddit/YouTube makes cold outreach feel familiar

**Case 4 – Feedback Loop Speed:**
- Switch to real-time dashboards; check weekly minimum; flag issues within 48 hours
